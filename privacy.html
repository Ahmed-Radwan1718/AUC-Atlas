AUC Atlas seeks to collect only personal data reasonably necessary for specified purposes.

When you create or maintain an account, AUC Atlas may process your full name, AUC email address, AUC ID number, telephone number, major, profile photograph, internal user ID, email-verification status, authentication provider, account timestamps, and account-security settings.

AUC Atlas may maintain normalized or lookup versions of your telephone number and AUC ID to prevent duplicate accounts and enforce account-uniqueness rules.

If you create a password-based account, password authentication is handled through the configured authentication provider. The AUC Atlas application database is not intended to store your plaintext password.

If you use a supported third-party sign-in provider such as Google, GitHub, or Facebook, AUC Atlas may receive information made available by that provider, such as your provider user identifier, name, email address, email-verification information, and profile photograph, depending on the provider and permissions you use.

AUC Atlas processes security information necessary to authenticate users, maintain login sessions, prevent unauthorized access, investigate abuse, and protect accounts.

This may include session identifiers, authentication cookies, login-challenge records, security-panel authorization records, authentication timestamps, IP addresses, user-agent information, browser name and version, operating system, device type, rate-limit counters, failed-attempt information, and security-event timestamps.

Current authenticated site sessions are designed to expire after approximately five days unless ended earlier through logout, revocation, account deletion, or another security event.

Short-lived login challenges and security-verification records may expire substantially sooner.

If you enable authenticator-app two-factor authentication, AUC Atlas may process encrypted authenticator secrets, temporary setup information, verification-attempt records, and whether two-factor authentication is enabled. Temporary authenticator setup sessions are designed to expire after a short period.

When you submit a professor review, AUC Atlas may process the professor, course code, semester, rating, recommendation information, attendance information, workload, lecture usefulness, office-hours experience, grading style, examination difficulty, grading transparency, feedback information, written comments, submission time, anonymous-status choice, and your internal account identifier.

If you submit a non-anonymous review, your display name and profile photograph may be displayed publicly with the review.

If you choose an anonymous review, AUC Atlas is designed not to display your account identity publicly with that review. However, your internal user identifier may still be retained privately so AUC Atlas can enforce review limits, allow account controls, investigate abuse, process reports, and comply with legal obligations.

An anonymous review therefore does not mean that AUC Atlas possesses no information capable of linking the review to the submitting account.

When you upload course material, AUC Atlas may process the file itself together with metadata such as course code, course title, professor, semester, material type, title, original file name, file size, file type or MIME type, file-storage identifier, upload group, upload time, moderation status, anonymous-status choice, uploader account identifier, uploader display name, and uploader profile photograph.

Course files may be stored with a third-party file-storage provider while associated metadata may be stored in AUC Atlas databases.

If an upload is non-anonymous, your display name and profile photograph may be displayed to other users with the material.

If an upload is anonymous, your public identity is hidden, but AUC Atlas may privately retain your account identifier and related technical records for moderation, security, deletion, legal compliance, and abuse prevention.

Before uploading files, remove unnecessary names, student IDs, emails, grades linked to identifiable individuals, private messages, medical information, financial information, identification documents, or other personal information that is not necessary for the legitimate academic purpose of the upload.

If you report a review or course material, AUC Atlas may process your internal user ID, verified AUC email address, the reported content identifier, your reason for reporting it, a snapshot of relevant reported content, timestamps, report status, and moderation or resolution information.

This information is used to investigate reports, prevent duplicate or abusive reports, communicate about serious issues where appropriate, maintain moderation records, protect users, enforce the Terms of Service, and establish or defend legal rights where necessary.

When you access AUC Atlas, technical information may be generated automatically by AUC Atlas and its infrastructure providers.

This may include IP address, date and time of requests, browser information, user-agent string, operating system, device type, requested pages or API endpoints, session information, security events, error information, network information, and information used to identify abusive or unusually frequent requests.

Hosting, authentication, storage, network, and security providers may independently generate technical logs as part of providing their services.

AUC Atlas currently generates a random identifier for non-admin visitors so that the Service can count unique visitors without using a person's name or email address for that purpose.

The identifier is stored in your browser's local storage under the key aucAtlasVisitorId. If local storage is unavailable, session storage may be used instead.

The identifier sent to the AUC Atlas server is hashed using SHA-256 before being stored in the unique-visitor database. The stored record may include the resulting hash and the time that identifier was first seen.

This information is used to maintain the Service's unique-visitor count and basic service analytics.

The visitor identifier is separate from your AUC Atlas account credentials. However, because persistent identifiers may constitute personal data where they can distinguish or relate to a person or device, AUC Atlas treats this information as protected data for purposes of this Notice where applicable.

AUC Atlas may use cookies, local storage, session storage, and similar browser mechanisms where reasonably necessary to provide authentication, account features, session security, login challenges, preferences, visitor counting, and other Service functionality.

Authentication and security cookies may contain random or cryptographically protected identifiers used to recognize a valid session or security challenge. AUC Atlas does not intend to place plaintext passwords inside browser cookies or local storage.

Where Egyptian law requires consent for a particular non-essential storage or tracking activity, AUC Atlas will obtain the legally required consent before using that mechanism.

AUC Atlas does not authorize service providers to use personal data for unrelated purposes merely because they receive data while providing infrastructure to AUC Atlas.

AUC Atlas is operated from the Arab Republic of Egypt but relies on technology providers whose infrastructure may store, access, transmit, cache, or otherwise process information outside Egypt.

Firebase Authentication: Google states that Firebase Authentication is operated exclusively from data centers in the United States. Authentication information processed through this service is therefore processed in the United States.

Cloud Firestore: AUC Atlas uses Cloud Firestore for database functionality. Firestore data is stored in the geographic location selected for the relevant Firebase database. That database location is an account-level configuration and is not contained in the public AUC Atlas source code.

Vercel: AUC Atlas uses Vercel for deployment, hosting, network delivery, and server-side functionality. The repository does not declare a custom Vercel Function region. Vercel documents Washington, D.C., United States as its default Function region, although account-level project settings may configure a different region. Static content may be delivered and temporarily cached through Vercel's global network.

Cloudinary: AUC Atlas uses Cloudinary for profile photographs. Cloudinary states that its standard/default storage data centers are located in the United States unless a different geographical data center has been specifically arranged. Content may also be distributed through global content-delivery infrastructure.

ImageKit: AUC Atlas uses ImageKit for course-material storage and delivery. ImageKit stores and processes uploaded files in the processing region selected for the relevant ImageKit account. Available processing regions include locations in the United States, Germany, India, Singapore, Australia, and Canada. Certain ImageKit account information, access logs, and server logs may be processed in the United States, and files may be cached through global content-delivery infrastructure.

Because the precise Firestore database location and ImageKit processing region are determined through account-level settings rather than the public source code, AUC Atlas will maintain and update the applicable location information based on the configurations actually used for those services.

Any transfer of personal data outside Egypt is subject to Egyptian Personal Data Protection Law No. 151 of 2020, its Executive Regulations, and applicable PDPC requirements. Where a license, permit, authorization, consent, safeguard, adequacy condition, or other legal transfer mechanism is required, AUC Atlas will comply with that requirement to the extent applicable to the relevant processing.

AUC Atlas will update this Notice when a material provider, storage location, processing location, or international-transfer arrangement changes where notice is required by applicable law.

AUC Atlas will not intentionally retain identifiable personal data longer than reasonably necessary for the lawful purpose for which it was processed, except where a longer period is required or permitted by law.

Retention criteria include the type of data, purpose of processing, account status, security requirements, moderation requirements, legal obligations, dispute or limitation periods, and whether the information remains necessary to operate the feature requested by the user.

  • Account and profile data may generally be retained while the account remains active and until deletion is completed, subject to lawful exceptions.
  • Authentication sessions are currently designed to expire after approximately five days unless terminated earlier.
  • Login challenges, authenticator setup sessions, and similar temporary security data are generally designed to expire after substantially shorter periods.
  • Rate-limit and anti-abuse information may be retained for the applicable security window and, where necessary, for a longer period to investigate serious abuse or defend legal rights.
  • Professor reviews and course materials may remain available until deleted by the user where available, removed through moderation, deleted with the associated account, or otherwise no longer necessary.
  • Content-report and moderation records may be retained for as long as reasonably necessary to resolve reports, prevent repeated abuse, maintain an appropriate moderation record, or establish or defend legal claims.
  • The hashed unique-visitor identifier may be retained while it remains necessary to maintain the all-time unique-visitor counting function. If that purpose ends, the associated record should be deleted or irreversibly anonymized unless another lawful retention basis applies.
  • Backup copies may persist for a limited technical period after deletion until overwritten or removed through normal backup processes.

Independent service providers may maintain their own technical or security records in accordance with their legal obligations and applicable retention rules.

AUC Atlas provides an account-deletion process for authenticated users.

Current account deletion is designed to remove the user's authentication account and associated AUC Atlas user record, profile photograph stored through the configured profile-photo provider, course-material records and associated stored files belonging to that user, professor reviews linked to that user, upload authorization records, account phone and AUC-ID reservation records, two-factor authentication records, certain rate-limit records, and other account-linked records handled by the deletion process.

Deletion may not remove information that AUC Atlas is legally required or permitted to retain, information that has been irreversibly anonymized, information contained temporarily in backups, or copies independently obtained by third parties from information that was public before deletion.

AUC Atlas may retain limited records where necessary to comply with law, investigate serious abuse, establish or defend legal rights, respond to lawful authorities, or satisfy another lawful retention requirement.
